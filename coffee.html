Coffee Lover

Life is too short for average coffee. Here are the spots worth your time.

From a strong flat white before the morning commute to a long black on a slow weekend — coffee has always been more than a drink to me. This page collects the cafés I return to, the ones that get the craft right. Starting with Sydney, and growing from there.

☕ Sydney, NSW — Australia

Diggy Doos Coffee

Bridge St, CBD

Lo-fi and completely honest about it — mismatched stools, handmade ceramics, spent coffee bags from guest roasters pinned to the walls. Near Wynyard and feels like a room in someone’s house. Paul Lee, formerly of Proud Mary Melbourne, runs it with quiet precision. The daily blackboard cycles through single-origins and geisha lots that are worth talking to the barista about.

Must try: Whatever is on the blackboard — single-origin, often geisha, always worth asking about.

Only Coffee Project

Willoughby Rd, Crows Nest

A minimalist espresso bar that takes transparency seriously — tasting cards list the exact price paid for the beans at origin. Market Lane from Melbourne handles the milk drinks; Sey Coffee, a prestigious New York micro-roastery, covers the black and filter side. Filter is served in Origami ceramic cups designed to open up the aroma. One of the most education-forward cafés in Sydney.

Must try: Flat white on Market Lane, or ask for the Sey Coffee filter if you want to understand what specialty coffee actually means.

Kingswood Coffee

George St, CBD

Old-fashioned in the best way — wood panelling, polished brass, and a space designed by Tamsin Johnson that draws from the original World Square building’s character. The Steadfast blend from Sensory Lab anchors the milk-based menu, with a rotating monthly guest roaster on espresso and filter. Pastries from Penny Fours, sourdough from Brickfields. The kind of café that quietly raises your standard for every cup after it.

Must try: Flat white on the Steadfast blend — ask what the guest roaster is and order that on filter too.
