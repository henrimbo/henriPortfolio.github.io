The Passion Behind It

THV Surf Co didn't start as a business idea — it started in the water. Surfing was already a core part of my life long before the brand existed. Hours in the ocean reading swells, understanding conditions, feeling what gear worked and what didn't. That knowledge wasn't academic — it was lived.

When you're a surfer building a surf brand, your customers aren't strangers. They're you. You know exactly what the product needs to feel like, perform like, and look like — because you've needed it yourself. That authenticity was THV's biggest advantage, and no amount of market research could have replicated it.

The ocean also taught me things that translate well beyond surfing — reading situations quickly, committing fully when the moment comes, knowing when to hold back, and accepting that sometimes conditions just don't go your way. Lessons that show up in every boardroom and every spreadsheet.

The Story

Porto Alegre sits in the south of Brazil, where winters are genuinely cold — low temperatures, rough swells, and water that makes you want to get warm and dry as quickly as possible after a session. In 2015, there was a clear gap in the market: very few brands were producing quality surf ponchos locally, and the large international labels that did sell them were priced out of reach for most everyday surfers.

A surf poncho is a full-length hooded towel designed to dry off and change after surfing while staying warm — a simple product, but a real and daily need for anyone who surfs in cold conditions. Two friends and I saw that gap and decided to build a brand around it: local, quality-focused, and priced honestly compared to the major surf labels.

We started the way most grassroots brands do — I sold the first ponchos directly to surfers from my car at the beach. No retail presence, no website, just showing up where the customers were and letting the product speak for itself. From there, the brand grew into wholesale partnerships with local surf shops and a presence at surf and skate tournaments that also attracted sponsorship from brands such as Red Bull. Being at those events alongside established names gave THV genuine credibility and exposure that no advertising spend could have bought.

It was never just a hobby. THV operated as a real business — with supplier contracts, stock management, margin tracking, marketing campaigns, and customer relationships to maintain. Running it alongside other work meant every hour counted and every decision had a direct consequence. That kind of pressure teaches you quickly.

Timeline

15
2015 — Founded

The Idea, the Product, the First Sale

Two friends and I identified a clear gap in southern Brazil: quality surf ponchos were scarce locally, and the big brands that produced them charged prices most surfers could not justify. We built THV Surf Co around that opportunity — sourcing manufacturers, designing the product line, and making the first sales directly to surfers from the car at the beach.

16
2016 — Distribution & Retail

Wholesale Into Local Surf Shops

Expanded from direct sales into wholesale partnerships with local surf shops. Negotiated pricing, payment terms, and consignment arrangements. Learned firsthand how margins work across a supply chain and how to maintain relationships when things do not go to plan.

16
2016 – 2017 — Brand Building

Tournament Sponsorships & Community Presence

Grew the brand through sponsorship of local surf and skate tournaments — events that also attracted support from Red Bull and other established names. That exposure placed THV alongside major brands and built genuine recognition in the local surf community. Product photography, social media, and grassroots engagement ran alongside the event presence.

17
2017 — Operations

Tightening the Supply Chain and Finances

Improved supplier lead times, refined the inventory model to reduce dead stock, and implemented basic financial tracking across margins, costs, and cash flow. Began thinking seriously about the next chapter and which skills would transfer.

18
2018 — Decision & Transition

Closing the Chapter, Opening the Next

After four years of building, learning, and growing, I made the decision to pursue a new challenge — relocating to Australia to invest in further education in data analytics. THV had given me the foundation; it was time to build something new on top of it.

What THV Surf Co Taught Me

Running a business solo, with limited resources and no safety net, compressed years of professional development into a short time. Every function I later saw in corporate roles — I had already done it myself.

"Building THV was not about becoming a fashion entrepreneur. It was about proving — to myself, mostly — that I could take something from nothing and make it real. Selling ponchos from a car boot at the beach taught me more about reading customers and earning trust than any course ever could. The skills I built running that brand: negotiating, managing cash, building relationships, executing under pressure — transferred directly into every professional role since. The brand is gone, but the foundation it built is not." — Henrique Borges, on the THV experience

The Next Chapter
